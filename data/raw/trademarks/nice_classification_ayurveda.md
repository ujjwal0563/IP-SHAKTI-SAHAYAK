# Nice Classification (NCL) Guide for Ayurvedic Products

**Authority:** World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) / Trade Marks Registry (India)
**Jurisdiction:** IN
**Category:** TRADEMARK
**Subject:** Classifying Ayurvedic and Herbal Products for Trademark Registration

## Overview of the Nice Classification
The Nice Classification (NCL) is an international classification of goods and services applied for the registration of marks. When applying for a Trademark for an Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani, or general herbal product, the applicant must file under the correct "Class". Incorrect classification can lead to application rejection or insufficient protection.

## Primary Classes for Ayurvedic Products

### Class 5: Pharmaceuticals and Medical Preparations
This is the primary and most important class for classical Ayurvedic drugs, proprietary medicines, and phytopharmaceuticals.
- **Includes:** 
  - Ayurvedic, Unani, and Siddha medicines.
  - Herbal and dietary supplements intended for medical purposes.
  - Medicinal herbal extracts.
  - Medicated oils, medicated soaps, and medicated shampoos.
  - Dietetic food and substances adapted for medical or veterinary use.
- **Excludes:** Non-medicated cosmetics, non-medicated dietary supplements (if classified solely as food).

### Class 3: Non-Medicated Cosmetics and Toiletry Preparations
If an Ayurvedic product is marketed primarily for cosmetic, beautification, or cleansing purposes without explicit medical claims, it falls here.
- **Includes:** 
  - Non-medicated herbal cosmetics.
  - Essential oils and herbal hair oils (non-medicated).
  - Herbal soaps, shampoos, and lotions.
  - Herbal dentifrices (toothpastes).
- **Excludes:** Medicated soaps and shampoos (Class 5).

### Class 29 & Class 30: Food Products and Ayurveda Aahar
For Ayurvedic dietary supplements, nutritional products, and "Ayurveda Aahar" that are marketed as food rather than medicine.
- **Class 29 Includes:** Preserved, dried, and cooked fruits/vegetables, jellies, jams, milk and milk products, edible oils (e.g., specific herbal infused cooking oils).
- **Class 30 Includes:** Coffee, tea, cocoa, herbal teas (non-medicated), spices, honey, treacle, and specific grain preparations.

### Class 32: Non-Alcoholic Beverages
- **Includes:** Herbal juices, aloe vera drinks (non-medicated), and herbal energy drinks.

## Strategy for Ayurvedic Innovators
If a classical Ayurvedic formulation cannot be patented due to Section 3(p) of the Patents Act, securing a strong Trademark under the appropriate Nice Class (typically Class 5) is the recommended alternative strategy to build brand equity and protect commercial interests. For multi-use products (e.g., a herbal shampoo that also claims to treat dandruff), it is often recommended to file a multi-class trademark application across both Class 3 and Class 5.
