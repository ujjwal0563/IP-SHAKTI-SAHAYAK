# The Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023
**Official Code:** Act No. 18 of 2023  
**Authority:** Ministry of Law and Justice (Legislative Department)  
**Jurisdiction:** IN  
**Category:** REGULATORY  

---

### Section 1 - Decriminalisation and Rationalisation of Offences
The Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023 was enacted by the Parliament of India to decriminalise and rationalise offences to further enhance trust-based governance for ease of living and doing business.
Under Section 3, fines and penalties provided under various provisions shall be increased by ten per cent of the minimum amount after the expiry of every three years from commencement.

---

### Section 2 - Major Amendments to The Patents Act, 1970 (Schedule Entry 18)
The Act amends key penal and enforcement provisions of the Indian Patents Act, 1970:
- **Section 120 (Unauthorized claim of patent rights):** For the words "punishable with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees", substituted by "liable to penalty which may extend to **ten lakh rupees**, and in case of continuing claim, a further penalty of **one thousand rupees per day**".
- **Section 121:** Omitted.
- **Section 122(1) (Refusal or failure to furnish information):** Substituted with "liable to penalty which may extend to **one lakh rupees**, and in case of continuing refusal or failure, a further penalty of **one thousand rupees per day**".
- **Section 122(2) (Furnishing false information/statements):** Imprisonment up to 6 months is **decriminalised and replaced** by civil penalty: "liable to penalty for a sum equal to **one half per cent (0.5%) of the total sale or turnover** of business or gross receipts in profession, or a sum equal to **five crore rupees**, whichever is less".
- **Section 123 (Practice by non-registered patent agents):** Decriminalised to a civil penalty up to **five lakh rupees**, plus **one thousand rupees per day** for continuing default.
- **Section 124A (Adjudication of Penalties):** The Controller of Patents may authorise an officer to be an adjudicating officer to hold inquiries and impose penalties after giving reasonable opportunity of being heard.
- **Section 124B (Appeals):** Any person aggrieved by an order under Section 124A may prefer an appeal to an appellate authority (at least one rank above adjudicating officer) within **sixty days**.

---

### Section 3 - Major Amendments to The Trade Marks Act, 1999 (Schedule Entry 30)
- **Section 106:** Omitted.
- **Section 107(2) (Falsely representing a trade mark as registered):** Imprisonment up to 3 years is **decriminalised** and replaced by: "liable to penalty of a sum equal to **one half per cent (0.5%) of total sales or turnover** in business or gross receipts, or a sum equal to **five lakh rupees**, whichever is less".
- **Sections 108 and 109:** Omitted.
- **Section 112A (Adjudication of Penalties):** The Registrar of Trade Marks may authorise an officer to be an adjudicating officer for holding an inquiry and imposing penalties.
- **Section 112B (Appeals):** Appeal against an adjudicating officer's order may be preferred to an appellate authority within **sixty days**.
- **Section 140(3):** Importer failure penalty set at **ten thousand rupees**.

---

### Section 4 - Major Amendments to The Geographical Indications of Goods Act, 1999 (Schedule Entry 31)
- **Section 37A (Adjudication of Penalties):** The Registrar of Geographical Indications may authorise an officer to act as adjudicating officer to hold inquiries and impose penalties.
- **Section 37B (Appeals):** Appeals against penalty orders may be preferred within **sixty days** to the appellate authority.
- **Section 42(2) (Falsely representing a geographical indication as registered):** Imprisonment up to 3 years is **decriminalised** and replaced by: "liable to penalty of a sum equal to **one-half per cent (0.5%) of total sales or turnover**, or **five lakh rupees**, whichever is less".
- **Sections 43 and 44:** Omitted.

---

### Section 5 - Major Amendments to The Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 (Schedule Entry 6)
- **Section 29:** Penalty for use of Government Analyst reports for advertising increased: "liable to penalty which may extend to **one lakh rupees**" (replacing fine up to 5,000 rupees).
- **Section 30(2):** Second/subsequent offences: Imprisonment removed for certain technical offences and substituted with: "fine which shall not be less than **five lakh rupees**".
- **Section 32B(1):** Compounding of offences expanded to include clause (d) of section 27 and clause (ii) of section 27A.
